## Design Context

### Users

Still Shift helps creators turn prepared stills into deterministic motion with low manual repair. Its current History Offstage consumer is a narrated illustrated documentary for curious English-speaking adults interested in ordinary life and historical systems. Episode graphics must communicate on phones as well as larger displays.

This context governs the **History Offstage motion collection**. Its palette, typography and illustration rules should not be imposed on unrelated commerce templates or treated as a redesign of the lab interface.

### Brand Personality

Curious, expressive, trustworthy. Explain ordinary people's practical circumstances with contemporary editorial clarity and visible limits of evidence. The user explicitly finds the seven motion behaviors useful and requests a more aesthetic graphic treatment (2026-09-26).

### Aesthetic Direction

The existing channel authority is Layered Chronicle Animation v1.1: expressive hand-drawn forms, shallow layered depth, restrained print texture, modern editorial composition and visible sourcing. Preserve its canonical bone, ink, field, grain, red and crisis palette. Typography: Source Serif 4, IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono, with final 1080p text no smaller than 42 px.

The owner requests continuous storytelling motion: the camera carries the idea, currents sustain relationships and subjects respond to change. Titles and qualifications stay readable and screen-locked; fixed brush texture never boils. See [the continuous storytelling plan](docs/story-motion-continuous-storytelling-plan.md).

Avoid generic presentation layouts, unrelated clip-art styles, fake parchment/archives, faux woodcut imitation, glossy 3D, tiny labels and decorative motion. Generated illustrations must remain distinct from authentic evidence.

The user authorized implementation of the complete visual pass on 2026-09-26. It uses larger focal subjects, distinct scene compositions and a coherent reusable vector family within the existing channel identity. This authorizes implementation; it does not record a human creative/release acceptance of the rendered results.

### Design Principles

1. Design a finished held frame before adding movement. One clear idea carries each beat.
2. Reuse one illustration family and stable symbol meanings; vary staging, scale and context across shots.
3. Let motion explain a relationship. Preserve the existing seven recipes' semantic and exact-frame contracts.
4. Give labels, dates and evidence limits real space and contrast. Keep production metadata outside episode footage.
5. Use expressive split-nib brush connections with authored curves where space allows: broad pressure changes, irregular pigment edges and restrained dry streaks. The user found smooth tapered vectors too plain and requested a more artistic line treatment on 2026-09-26. Keep the entire stroke within its clearance envelope. Give drawing, arrivals and settling distinct timing; retain monotonic movement, readable text and meaningful exact cuts. The owner decision of 2026-09-26 supersedes frozen holds: every second must contain story-bearing motion. A hold is held in meaning, not frozen in pixels; use a continuous camera carrier, actions, responses and meaningful currents.
6. Improve beauty without increasing unsupported historical specificity. Maintain source lineage and distinguish unknown, symbolic and composite content.
7. Preserve a clear typographic hierarchy and illustration-led composition. On 2026-09-26 the user rejected the blanket 80 px enlargement because it increased overall crowding and weakened hierarchy. Use separate title, subheading, label and qualification roles; judge the whole composition before increasing text to satisfy a size warning. Numerical text-size checks do not establish readability or creative acceptance.
