# Story-motion research: Relationship Build and Motif Resolve

**Researched:** 2026-09-26 · **Status:** implementation preparation only
**Scope:** M2 and the Motif Resolve portion of M5 in the [S01E01 roadmap](s01e01-story-motion-roadmap.md). These are project recipe names, not established industry effect names. Sources below demonstrate reusable implementation techniques; they do not establish that the proposed historical scenes are accurate or engaging.

The recommended approach is to retain the current Canvas renderer and compile authored narrative events into deterministic tracks. Borrow progressive stroke reveal, overlapping event timing, and identity-preserving layout transitions from established tools. There is no demonstrated need to introduce GSAP, Manim, or Motion Canvas as a new production dependency.

## Existing engine: useful capabilities and real gaps

The inspected [prepared compiler](../packages/renderer-core/src/prepared-scene.ts) already produces tracks for position, scale, rotation, opacity, stroke reveal, gap, state and pulse. `sampleTrack` uses smoothstep interpolation, and `evaluatePreparedNode` samples absolute clip frames without depending on previous playback. The [Canvas renderer](../packages/renderer-core/src/illustrated-renderer.ts) supports nested group transforms and draws partial polylines by geometric length.

The [scene contract](../packages/scene-contract/src/prepared.ts) currently requires every `resource_flow` branch to have a path, travelling image token, and destination. Its compiler distributes events across a fixed seven-second template and scales all times by clip duration. Paths contain static point arrays; their endpoints are not bound to moving nodes. Group parents are static. Prepared v1 duration is limited to 3–8 seconds. ST-006 is 17 seconds, and several conclusion shots also exceed eight seconds. Recommend a new story contract with authoritative integer `frameCount`, leaving v1 unchanged; split clips only at authored narrative boundaries with explicit continuity. Simply stretching today's recipe is insufficient.

## Relationship Build

**Narrative purpose:** start with one understandable resource, then expose the relationships that make the household depend on more than that resource. First target: ST-006, followed by the ST-006–008 passage. Later applications include ST-009/010/016/025.

### Proposed event flow

1. **Establish:** hold the existing store at a readable scale. Its position becomes the viewer's reference. Keep surrounding relationships unexposed in the prepared artwork, which requires removing baked connectors and labels from the source derivative.
2. **First dependency:** as narration names it, reveal one appropriate connector and its destination group. A relationship label appears beside the connection, rather than in a disconnected sidebar.
3. **Broaden the explanation:** reveal the next narration-sized group while the first becomes secondary. Retain completed connections at readable lower emphasis; do not imply they have ceased to exist.
4. **Regroup:** compose the already seen relationships around the household/store so that the system, rather than a single commodity, becomes the point. Keep no more than three independently moving focal elements at once, following the roadmap's limit.
5. **Handoff:** hold the final system, then carry the store motif into ST-007. The next shot can introduce a qualitative loss condition without draining the grain level.

This is our staging proposal. The sources establish mechanics, not a guaranteed antidote to boredom. The meaningful development is the change from “one store” to “several kinds of dependence.” More particles or faster lines would not create that development.

### Prepared inputs and smallest implementation changes

- **Roles/assets:** stable `anchor`, `destinationGroup`, `relationshipPath`, adjacent `label`, and optional `qualifier`; cleaned store and household cutouts; matching background repairs; grouped resource/access/claim artwork. Relationships need a declared meaning and direction, including explicit nondirectional cases. Keep familiar IDs, crop registration, source hashes and ground anchors.
- **Timing:** each semantic event declares its clip-local start/end frames, changed roles, emphasis target and hold. Store the master-frame offset separately. Treat timing derived from narration as authored data.
- **Recipe addition:** add a token-free branch mode for the new `relationship_build` contract. Preserve old `resource_flow` token requirements and schedules. Reuse reveal, opacity and transform tracks instead of creating another renderer.
- **Track compilation:** support several events on one property by collecting and validating keys; today's `track()` helper replaces a property's track on each call. Reject conflicting same-frame changes rather than letting the last call win.
- **Geometry:** for the smallest first proof, keep connected nodes fixed while revealing paths. For actual regrouping, use a shared parent when the whole connected assembly moves rigidly. Where endpoints separate, add limited anchor-bound line geometry or authored equal-topology path states. Do not assume a static path will follow a moved cutout.
- **Emphasis:** opacity and existing group transforms are enough for a first proof. Add stroke width or color interpolation only if an observed readability problem warrants it; do not overload the existing fracture-style `pulse` as generic attention.

### Proof and QA

Build a 4–8-second fixture with a fixed store, two relationship groups, one emphasis transfer and a final hold. Then conform the actual ST-006 passage. Verify sequential playback and random seeks give identical role states and rendered frames at event start, midpoint, end and adjacent frames. Compare lab and export at the same exact frame indices, with identical asset/font availability.

Geometry checks must include zero-length paths, repeated vertices, 0/100% reveal, parent scale/rotation, line endpoints touching the intended anchor, and absence of duplicated baked paths. If endpoints animate, test their screen-space agreement throughout movement rather than only the destination pose. Phone-size playback must retain the label-to-object association and show which relationship changed.

**Failure modes:** meaningless token traffic that makes all edges look like food transport; an animated balance that implies prices; revealing every branch before the narration needs it; perpetual line pulsing; fading earlier dependencies until they seem absent; a 60-second radial board with repeated label entrances. For ST-006 specifically, do not send grain toward credit hands or animate the depicted balance as a measured quantity. These scene constraints come from the [motion study](../prompt-packs/s01e01-motion-design.md).

## Motif Resolve

**Narrative purpose:** bring back known objects, rearrange their relationships into the answer, then isolate the land → rent/service relationship for the next episode. Targets: ST-023/025–028; reuse the household comparison in ST-026 without treating its houses as newly discovered cases.

### How others implement the relevant techniques

| Primary precedent                                                                                                                                                        | Observed implementation                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Proposed Still Shift adaptation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [GSAP Flip: capture/change/animate example and matching IDs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/Flip/)                                                                     | The worked example captures element geometry, changes the layout, then animates from the captured state. `data-flip-id` pairs corresponding objects across layouts; nested transform compensation prevents compounded offsets. | Represent familiar motifs with stable semantic IDs and named start/end layouts. Compile transform tracks between those authored poses. Since Canvas has no DOM layout to measure, precomputed coordinates are simpler than importing Flip. Treat parent/world-coordinate changes explicitly. |
| [Manim TransformMatchingShapes: Anagram example](https://docs.manim.community/en/stable/reference/manim.animation.transform_matching_parts.TransformMatchingShapes.html) | The example transforms one phrase into another by moving matched letter shapes along arcs. Matching uses normalized point-coordinate hashes, rather than semantic meaning.                                                     | Borrow the continuity of visible parts, but match our raster motifs by explicit semantic ID. Two identical household silhouettes can represent different households; visual similarity is not a safe identity key. Keep image contents intact while changing position or hierarchy.          |
| [Remotion: frame-driven property animation](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/animating-properties)                                                                          | Its fade example derives opacity from the current frame and clamps interpolation. The guide warns that animation driven outside the frame model, such as CSS transitions, can flicker during rendering.                        | Encode each motif's full pose as a function of clip frame. Carry an explicit outgoing pose into the next scene's initial pose; never require the previous clip to have played first. This aligns with the existing prepared evaluator and the episode's Remotion assembly.                   |

### Proposed event flow

1. **Recognition:** reuse the familiar household silhouettes, resource kit, baseline and claims. Their visual identity signals that the conclusion is assembling what the viewer already learned.
2. **Synthesis:** ST-025 groups resources, access and claims as they are spoken. Most components begin present or return through a short matched transition. This is not a second full branch-by-branch introduction.
3. **Return to the comparison:** ST-026 uses the earlier paired anchors and shared season baseline. The surrounding explanation is richer; the two households do not receive different weather.
4. **Narrow the question:** ST-027 moves the calendar into secondary emphasis, keeps resources and claims readable, and isolates the land → rent/service relationship. The outgoing relationship receives space, rather than acquiring extra particles or new numerical detail.
5. **Close:** ST-028 holds the intact threshold and a separate next-episode region for its approximately 4.708 seconds. Do not manufacture an action at the door to fill the hold.

### Prepared inputs and smallest implementation changes

- **Roles/assets:** recurring motifs and their semantic IDs; named composition poses; outgoing relationship and label; persistent qualifier; safe end-screen rectangle; intact threshold artwork. Record which earlier appearance each motif recalls. Keep identity distinct from asset ID so two roles can share an image without becoming interchangeable.
- **Pose contract:** store entry and exit position, rotation, scale, opacity and relevant image state. Bake transforms in one known coordinate space. Prefer a static parent hierarchy throughout each transition; reparenting is unnecessary for the first implementation.
- **Compiler:** reuse x/y/scale/opacity tracks with authored hold points. Distinguish matched motifs, entering motifs and exiting motifs. Keep the first implementation to straight movement or an authored waypoint; a spline system and automatic shape matching are unnecessary.
- **Boundary contract:** serialize the outgoing pose and asset/crop registration for the incoming clip. Match-cut when the object is unchanged; use an explicitly authored transition when its illustration differs. Do not silently morph one historical object into another because their silhouettes resemble each other.
- **Connectors:** when two endpoints regroup independently, use the same bounded anchor-geometry support identified for Relationship Build. A path hidden during movement and redrawn afterward is acceptable only if the editorial treatment intentionally re-establishes the relationship and does not suggest its interruption.

### Proof and QA

Start with a 4–8-second fixture: familiar household + three grouped factors → factors settle → one outgoing relation remains emphasized. Test a matched clip boundary using the same asset and a deliberately different crop registration to confirm that validation catches a visible jump. Measure continuity in world/screen coordinates, including scale and opacity, not just equal local x/y values.

Seek directly into every regrouping event, replay in reverse, and compare the result with sequential playback. Check collision and occlusion at midpoint poses, especially when silhouettes exchange ordering; final-pose checks alone miss crossing collisions. Compare preview/export frames around the match boundary. Review phone-size playback and verify the end-screen region remains clear. Preserve a genuinely still final hold once the conclusion is readable.

**Failure modes:** restarting the full resource reveal; matching by shape or file name instead of role; moving all motifs at once; double-applying parent and child offsets; connectors left behind; shrinking qualifiers to illegibility; using a calendar disappearance to imply season has no importance; adding a threshold closure, punishment or eviction that the narration never states. The conclusion should consolidate prior meaning, not introduce a new historical claim.

## Evidence limits and implementation decision

The six external references were inspected as first-party documentation and worked example code. No referenced animation was locally rendered or temporally reviewed for this note. Their suitability for S01E01 is an engineering and editorial inference, to be tested in the short proofs above.

Proceed with shared event compilation, explicit identity/pose continuity, and limited anchor-bound geometry. Keep the existing renderer and legacy recipe behavior. Defer automatic layout, shape morphing, token simulations and additional easing libraries until a real proof demonstrates a specific unmet need.
